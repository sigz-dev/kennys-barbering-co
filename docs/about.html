Since 1998

Four chairs, one standard.

Twenty-eight years on the same corner of Fourth Avenue. Here’s how a one-chair shop became the reason people drive across Johannesburg for a haircut.

Our story

A borrowed mirror and a secondhand chair.

In 1998 Kenny Mareya took a lease on a narrow unit at the quiet end of Fourth Avenue. He had one chair, a borrowed mirror, a hand-painted sign, and a rule he still repeats to every apprentice: a good cut should still look like a good cut four weeks later.

Parkhurst changed around him. The strip filled with restaurants, the rents climbed, and a dozen shops opened and closed within walking distance. Kenny stayed on the same corner, cutting mostly with scissors, taking the first appointment of the day.

The shop grew by exactly one chair at a time — and only when there was someone good enough to fill it. Every barber on our floor came up through our own apprenticeship, which is the whole reason the standard holds whichever chair you land in.

The long version

Twenty-eight years, five moments

  1. 1998

    One chair, one mirror

    Kenny Mareya opens a single-chair shop on Fourth Avenue with a secondhand barber's chair and a hand-painted sign.

  2. 2006

    The second chair

    Demand outgrows one pair of hands. The shop takes over the unit next door and the apprenticeship programme begins.

  3. 2015

    Thabo joins

    Thabo Nkosi starts as an apprentice, sweeping floors and learning fades. He's run the second chair since 2019.

  4. 2021

    The shave returns

    Rea Dlamini brings traditional straight razor wet shaving to the shop, and the Hot Towel Shave becomes a booking in its own right.

  5. 2026

    Four chairs, same standard

    Twenty-eight years on Fourth Avenue. Same corner, same founder, still cutting the first appointment of the day.

What we hold to

How we work

Four things we don’t compromise on, however busy the shop gets.

01

Consultation first

No chair time starts until we've agreed on what we're doing. Bring a photo, bring an idea, or bring nothing at all — we'll work it out together before the clippers come out.

02

Time to do it properly

We book in forty-five minute blocks where most shops book thirty. It means we never rush the blend, and it means you're never waiting on the chair in front of you.

03

Trained in-house

Every barber on our floor came up through our own apprenticeship. It's why the standard is the same whichever chair you land in.

04

A cut that grows out well

Anyone can make hair look good on the day. We cut with week four in mind, so it softens rather than falls apart.
